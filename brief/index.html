The story

If last week were a restaurant order, our demo athlete didn’t get the light salad — they got the tasting menu and asked for extra bread. Sleep averaged a healthy 7.2h, like keeping the phone on overnight charge instead of living at 12% battery… except 2025-04-08 (~5.8h), which is the classic “one short night that makes the next day feel like Windows Update.”

Steps ran warm (~9,787/day). For perspective: many step challenges treat 10k as the vanity badge; WHO cares more that adults clear ~150–300 min/week of moderate activity (or 75–150 vigorous). Tennis alone was ~5.5 hours on court across 5 sessions — denser than a typical recreational template of 2–3 sessions / ~2–3 hours a week. At a club / league-adjacent (illustrative) level, volume actually taxes legs and focus, not just vibes.

Resting HR stuck around ~56. Untrained adults often cruise the 60s–70s; regular tennis players in research often land lower. The useful self-awareness bit: RHR stayed roughly flat while load moved ~58% vs the prior month. That may mean the week was absorbed okay; it does not mean cloning the week forever is free.

What this may hint

  • Flat-ish RHR + a busier tennis week may suggest fitness is carrying the load — still watch how legs, mood, and sleep feel 24–48h after stacked days.
  • One short night mid-week may be noise (late match, travel, “one more YouTube”) rather than a new pattern — unless it starts chaining.
  • A step bump may be mostly court footsteps wearing a pedometer costume, not proof more cardio homework is required.
  • A ~58% load swing vs the prior month is coach-speak for “fun week — don’t photocopy it twice.” Recovery isn’t laziness; it’s how recreational players keep hitting clean balls in week three.

General wellness notes

Guidelines for a recreational adult tennis player — not a diagnosis, not a training plan, and not tied to any real person.

  • Sleep: National Sleep Foundation–style guidance for adults stays ~7–9h. Protect the night after a stacked match day like it’s a meeting with your future self.
  • Activity: WHO 2020 — move most days; add muscle work 2+ days/week. Many tennis humans are cardiorespiratory-rich; the missing subplot is often legs/hips strength + a true easy day.
  • Load: sports-science folk often treat sudden week-to-week spikes as the “blister and cranky shoulder” zone. A dense block isn’t catastrophic, but cloning it twice in a row is how “I’m fine” becomes “why does my elbow have opinions.”
  • Tennis lens: at club / recreational–intermediate level, quality & recovery beat grinding hours for ego. One focused session often beats a fourth exhausted one where every forehand is a negotiation.
